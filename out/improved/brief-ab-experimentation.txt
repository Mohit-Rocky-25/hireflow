PROBLEM STATEMENT
----------------------------------------
Fixed-horizon frequentist A/B testing requires accumulating a predetermined sample size before evaluating outcomes, prolonging exposure to suboptimal variants and encouraging premature stopping errors (peeking). When teams inspect p-values continuously without formal alpha-spending adjustments, false positive rates escalate significantly. A Bayesian experimentation engine addresses this by maintaining continuous posterior probability distributions over conversion rates, calculating the exact probability that a candidate variant outperforms the baseline and quantifying the expected financial or metric loss of selecting that variant, enabling automated stopping decisions while bounding decision risk.

USE CASES IN THE REAL WORLD
----------------------------------------
Online experimentation platforms evaluate user interface changes, pricing tiers, and checkout workflow variants across high-traffic digital applications. Neutral architectural reference points include web and mobile experimentation platforms such as Optimizely, Statsig, and GrowthBook.

WHY THIS IS HARD (COMPETENCY REDUCER)
----------------------------------------
Preventing False Positive Inflation Under Continuous Inspection: While Bayesian updating is logically coherent under sequential data arrivals, naive early stopping on uncalibrated thresholds (P(B > A) > 0.95) during low-sample cold starts (N < 200) causes severe discovery bias. The engine enforces minimum statistical power thresholds and computes Expected Loss thresholds rather than relying purely on tail probabilities.
